About

I'm a Network Operations Engineer who spends his days on the sharp end of infrastructure: alarm storms, carrier outages, and root-cause analysis across Cisco, Juniper, and Alcatel-Lucent platforms serving more than 100,000 subscribers. The job is, at its core, a data problem — separate the one failing circuit from ten thousand healthy ones, fast.

That instinct pulled me toward data science. I'm completing a Master of Data Science and building a portfolio of projects that show the full workflow: framing a question, wrangling messy data, choosing the right model, and communicating a result a non-technical stakeholder can act on. I care more about honest, well-scoped analysis than flashy accuracy numbers — especially when the cost of a wrong answer is real.

I'm looking for a Data Analyst or analytics-focused NOC Engineer role where I can pair operational experience with statistical rigor.

Supervised ML · Regression Complete

Beer Strength (ABV) Prediction

Predicts a beer's alcohol by volume from sensory review scores (malty, hoppy, bitter, body) and characteristics (style, IBU range) across ~3,200 beers. Compares Lasso regression against a Random Forest inside scikit-learn pipelines, with leakage prevention and a square-root target transform to address skew.

Result: Random Forest was the stronger model with a mean absolute error of 0.84 percentage points of ABV versus 1.40 points for Lasso — on the original ABV scale, not a percentage of error.

Supervised ML · Classification Complete

Mushroom Edibility Classification

Predicts whether a mushroom is edible or poisonous from observable physical traits, scoped to features a beginner forager could actually see in the field. Because calling a poisonous mushroom "edible" is a life-safety error, the project optimizes recall on the poisonous class and tunes the decision threshold toward caution instead of chasing raw accuracy.

Result: A Random Forest reliably separates the two classes; threshold tuning trades a few false alarms for near-zero missed-poisonous cases, and a "not certain" output handles low-confidence predictions.
